The SPF and PA numbers on a label tell you the protection a product is designed for. They do not tell you how the formula gets there. That depends on the **UV filter system** — which filters are used, and how they behave once they are on your skin in the sun.

## What a UV Filter Does

A UV filter is an ingredient that absorbs, scatters or reflects ultraviolet light before it reaches living skin cells. Filters fall into two broad groups:

- **Organic filters** absorb UV energy and release it as a small amount of heat.
- **Mineral filters** — zinc oxide and titanium dioxide — scatter and absorb UV.

Each filter works across a particular part of the UV range. Some cover mostly **UVB** (290–320 nm), the band behind sunburn. Some cover **UVA** (320–400 nm), the band linked to tanning, pigmentation and photoageing. A few cover both.

## What Photostability Means

When an organic filter absorbs UV, its molecule is briefly energised. A **photostable** filter returns to its original state and keeps working. A filter that is not photostable can gradually break down in sunlight, so the protection it offers can fall over time unless the formula stabilises it.

This is why formulators pay close attention to which filters they combine, not just how many.

## Why More Than One Filter

No single filter covers the whole UV range evenly. A filter system is built so that the strengths of one cover the gaps of another:

- broad-spectrum filters that reach across UVB and UVA,
- a dedicated UVA filter to strengthen the long-wave UVA end,
- a dedicated UVB filter,
- and mineral filters that add scattering across both bands.

Combining classes this way is how a formula reaches both a high SPF and a high PA designation.

## Daily Shield's Filter System

Daily Shield, an SPF 50 PA++++ SPF moisturizer, uses **six UV filters**: two broad-spectrum organic filters from the triazine class, a dedicated UVA filter, a dedicated UVB filter, and two mineral filters — zinc oxide and titanium dioxide. Each was chosen for stability in sunlight and for its place in the waveband coverage.

You can see which part of the UV range each filter class covers on the [science page](/science).

## What This Means When You Choose an SPF

The filter system is only half the story. The other half is how much you apply. SPF is tested with two finger-lengths of cream for the face and neck, and most people use about half that. Our [dose guide](/dose-guide) shows what the full amount looks like.
