Some of the most useful ingredients in modern skin care were not discovered in a lab designed for skin care at all. Ectoin is one of them. It comes from microbiology, not cosmetic chemistry, and its original job had nothing to do with human skin.

## An Ingredient Born in Extreme Environments

Ectoin belongs to a class of compounds called **extremolytes** — molecules produced by extremophile organisms, bacteria that live in conditions most life cannot tolerate: salt lakes, desert soil and other environments defined by heat, dryness or high salt.

These bacteria build up Ectoin inside their cells to keep their water balance under that stress. It surrounds proteins and cell membranes with a structured layer of water, which helps them keep their shape.

## What It Does on Skin

In skin care, Ectoin works the same way, on our own skin cells. **Ectoin draws water to skin cells and forms a stabilising shell around them, which supports hydration retention.**

Two things it does *not* do are worth being clear about:

- **It does not absorb UV.** Ectoin is not a UV filter. In Daily Shield, UV protection comes from the six UV filters in the formula — Ectoin sits alongside them for hydration.
- **It works in the outer layers of the skin.** It does not reach the dermis.

## Why It Belongs in a Daily SPF Moisturizer

An SPF product is something you wear every day, through heat, air-conditioning and long hours outside. A formula that helps skin hold on to water through all of that is a better daily product to wear — and a product people are comfortable wearing is a product they keep wearing.

That is the reason Ectoin is in Daily Shield: not as a substitute for UV filters, but as the hydration partner to them.

Ectoin is the signature active of the Therazo range. In Daily Shield it is listed on the back of the pack with its percentage, alongside niacinamide, ceramides and vitamin C — so you can see exactly how much is there rather than taking the ingredient's name on trust.
